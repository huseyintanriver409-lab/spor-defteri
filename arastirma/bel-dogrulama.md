# Bel (lumbar extensors) – verification report

Method: all 39 cited PMIDs plus the retracted Xu 2022 were pulled in one PubMed esummary + efetch (abstract) call on 2026-11-05. For every entry the authors, year, journal, volume/pages, DOI and title matched the citation. The Welch 2015 full text (Europe PMC, PMC5117021) was also read to check the fCSA claim, which is not in its abstract. Verdicts below rest on abstracts unless noted.

## Required corrections to B and C

**C1. Claim that the isolated pelvis-restrained machine is the best-evidenced tool for lumbar adaptation, citing [3] for size (C "Önemli uyarı", item 8, "Kanıt" list).**
This is supported for *ILEX strength* only, and even there the test-specificity bias in #16 applies. For *size* the evidence is mixed:
- [3] shows a gain, but combined with motor control and in LBP patients.
- [6] Willemink (isolated dynamic lumbar extensor machine) shows no significant fCSA change.
- [5] Berry (machine program) shows no change.
- [9] Käser "devices" group shows a slight decrease.

Reword to: "best evidence for isolated lumbar extension strength; hypertrophy evidence is mixed and comes only from LBP patients." Keep the suggestion to add a Lumbar Extension Machine at Bel 1.0 as inference.

**C2. "Kanıt" bullet: "RDL/squat/hip thrust/Roman chair izole lomber kuvveti artırmadı".**
Soften to "did not significantly increase isolated lumbar extension strength in small, short studies". The reasons:
- n=7 per group over 4 weeks with no control [17].
- 1 set once per week [19].
- Testing on the same machine the comparator group trained on [16].
- #16 and #18 share a sample.
- Almost all of these come from one research group (Southampton Solent: Fisher/Steele) or the Mayer group.

None measured muscle size, so the step to "the growth stimulus may be limited" must stay explicitly labelled inference. Evidence tier: weak to moderate, not settled.

**C3. Superman row cites [2] and [39] for "static holds possibly effective for multifidus".**
[2]'s static holds were inside an *intensive loaded lumbar resistance* program in LBP patients, and [39] is acute swelling only. Neither supports bodyweight superman growth. Remove them as support or mark the claim as inference. Superman 0.4 itself is fine; [24] and [26] are relevant EMG.

**C4. Good Morning raised to 0.7 because "EMG shows bel contribution close to deadlift" (C item 2).**
Neither [30] (within-GM load effect) nor [31] (ES direction not reported) compares GM ES to deadlift. Label 0.7 as inference (plausible from biomechanics: long trunk moment arm) and cite [22] only as general activation.

**C5. Danneels [2] in the "Kanıt" list ("statik tutuş eklenmesi multifidus büyümesini sağladı").**
State it as a single RCT in LBP patients with a within-group effect; the abstract gives no between-group test. Weak evidence, not established.

**C6. Hip Thrust 0.3.**
[28] found ES at 1RM *equal* to deadlift. The low weight rests on [17] (weak) plus an inference that the spine does not move. Keep 0.3 if you like, but label it inference and acknowledge [28] contradicts it.

**C7. Squat 0.4 versus Deadlift 0.8.**
[24] found lumbosacral ES higher in squat than deadlift at 80% 1RM, and [25] found them equal. The 2:1 gap is inference (deadlift's trunk lean and upper lumbar ES), not EMG-derived. The text partly admits this; make it explicit. 0.4–0.5 for squat is defensible.

**C8. Deadlift tier "Zayıf–orta".**
The supporting hypertrophy data are one uncontrolled LBP study with about 2–4% fCSA [1], a null comparison with no control [4], and cross-sectional athlete data [12, 13] that are confounded by selection. "Zayıf" is more accurate; "orta" only for the EMG claim.

**C9. Nuzzo [25] conclusion "SQs and DLs are recommended for hypertrophy of back extensors".**
This is the authors' opinion from an acute EMG study. If quoted in the app, label it as such.

No missed study was found that would change a conclusion. I am not aware of a longitudinal study in healthy people comparing back-muscle hypertrophy between exercises, which supports the researcher's main "no direct evidence" statement. This is unconfirmed beyond the searches already in the file.

## Overall judgment

All sources exist and their findings are reported accurately; none are fabricated or misrepresented. The main conclusions hold:
- There is no comparative hypertrophy data, so the weights are inference.
- Hinge, back extension and reverse hyper exercises activate the lumbar extensors highly.
- Compound lifts showed no significant ILEX strength transfer in small studies.
- Grouping exercises into "equal" groups is appropriate.

Fix the overstatements in C1 (ILEX machine and size), C2 (strength-transfer certainty), C3 (superman support) and C4 (good morning), and lower the deadlift hypertrophy tier to "weak". The numeric weights can stay, labelled as inference.
